PREFIRE V2: MASTER MULTI-AGENT EXECUTION PLAN
OBJECTIVE: Determine whether US wildfire suppression is truly becoming less
effective regardless of fire severity, or if apparent slowdowns are driven by
environmental difficulty, policy shifts, and concurrent resource saturation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: GROK BOT AGENT SPECIFICATION (MULTI-SOURCE DATA ACQUISITION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 AGENT ROLE & CONSTRAINTS
- Role: Autonomous Data Acquisition & Enrichment Agent.
- Time Budget: Complete all downloads, API queries, and schema validations
  within 15 minutes.
- Scraping Rule: Prefer REST APIs, bulk CSV/GeoPackage downloads, and structured
  HTML tables over headless browser automation. Use multi-threaded pagination
  for REST endpoints.

1.2 TARGET DATASETS TO ACQUIRE
Grok Bot must collect and standardize four distinct datasets spanning 1990–2026:

[DATASET 1: Core Wildfire Occurrences — USFS FOFL]
- Source: USDA Forest Service Enterprise Data Warehouse (EDW) / ArcGIS Hub.
- Primary REST Endpoint:
  https://apps.fs.usda.gov/arcx/rest/services/EDW/EDW_FireOccurrence_01/MapServer/0/query
- Backup Bulk Download:
  https://data.fs.usda.gov/geodata/edw/edw_resources/shp/S_USA.Fire_Occurrence.zip
- Filter Criteria: Fire Year >= 1990, Total Acres > 0, non-null Discovery and
  Fire Out timestamps, valid NAD83 Latitude (24.0 to 50.0) and Longitude
  (-125.0 to -66.0).
- Required Fields:
  * FIREYEAR (Integer)
  * DISCOVERYDATETIME (UTC Timestamp)
  * FIREOUTDATETIME (UTC Timestamp)
  * TOTALACRES (Float)
  * STATCAUSE (String/Code — to distinguish Lightning vs. Human ignitions)
  * LATDD83 (Float)
  * LONGDD83 (Float)
  * STATE / USFS REGION (String, if present)

[DATASET 2: Historical Fire Weather, Aridity & Terrain — Open-Meteo ERA5]
- Purpose: Acreage alone does not measure physical fire difficulty. A 50-acre
  fire in high wind, extreme atmospheric aridity, and steep terrain is much
  harder to suppress than a 50-acre fire in mild conditions.
- Source: Open-Meteo Historical Weather Archive API (ERA5 Reanalysis).
- Endpoint: https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive
- Spatial Strategy (To Avoid Rate Limits):
  Do NOT query Open-Meteo 500,000 individual times. Instead, group fire
  coordinates into 15 to 25 representative spatial grid centroids (or 2-degree
  lat/lon grid cells) covering the primary US fire ecoregions, and fetch daily
  historical series from 1990-01-01 to present for each centroid.
- Required Daily Variables & Metadata:
  * temperature_2m_max (Daily Max Temperature in Celsius)
  * wind_speed_10m_max (Daily Max Wind Speed in km/h)
  * precipitation_sum (Daily Precipitation in mm — used to compute 30-day
    rolling antecedent rainfall / drought deficit)
  * vapour_pressure_deficit_max (Daily Max VPD in kPa — primary physical driver
    of fuel flammability and fire spread rate)
  * elevation (Meters above sea level, returned in the API metadata payload)

[DATASET 3: Federal Suppression Costs & Operational Load — NIFC]
- Purpose: Test financial and resource efficiency (whether spending billions
  more per year is translating into faster containment).
- Source: National Interagency Fire Center (NIFC) Historical Wildland Fire
  Statistics & Suppression Cost tables (https://www.nifc.gov/fire-information/statistics).
- Required Annual Variables (1990–present):
  * Year
  * Total Federal Suppression Expenditure (USFS + DOI combined, in Nominal USD
    and CPI-adjusted Real USD)
  * Total National Wildland Fires and Acres reported by NIFC (for cross-check)

[DATASET 4: Human Proximity / Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) Context]
- Purpose: Fires near towns and roads receive aggressive immediate aerial and
  ground attack; remote wilderness fires are frequently monitored under resource-
  benefit ("let-burn") policies.
- Source: Derive distance from each fire's (LATDD83, LONGDD83) to the nearest
  populated place / major urban center using a lightweight US Census Gazetteer
  city/county centroid file or Natural Earth populated places table.

2. Validation Checks:

   - Zero NaN values in core coordinates, acreage, or discovery/out dates.

2.1 AGENT ROLE & ARCHITECTURE

2.2 PHASE 1: CONFOUNDER ISOLATION & FEATURE ENGINEERING (`src/clean_and_engineer.py`)
To scientifically test if suppression is getting worse regardless of severity,
Codex must engineer five strict control variables:

1. Target Metrics:
   - `SUPP_DAYS`: Exact duration in fractional days between Discovery and Out
     timestamps (filtered to valid range 0 to 365 days).
   - `IA_ESCAPED`: Binary indicator of Initial Attack failure (1 if `SUPP_DAYS > 3`
     or `TOTALACRES > 100`, else 0).

2. Control 1 — Fixed Fire Size Stratification (`SIZE_BIN`):
   - Categorize `TOTALACRES` into 5 strict buckets so size skew cannot distort
     temporal comparisons:
     * Micro (< 1 acre)
     * Small (1 to 10 acres)
     * Medium (10 to 100 acres)
     * Large (100 to 1,000 acres)
     * Megafire (> 1,000 acres)

3. Control 2 — Policy & Tactical Split (`CAUSE_TYPE` & `WUI_PROXIMITY`):
   - Classify `STATCAUSE` into `Human (Aggressive Suppression)` vs.
     `Lightning/Natural (Often Managed for Ecological Benefit)`.
   - Classify distance to populated centers into `WUI / Accessible` vs.
     `Remote Backcountry`.

4. Control 3 — Vectorized Sweep-Line Concurrency (`CONCURRENT_FIRES`):
   - Compute the exact number of simultaneous active fires burning nationwide
     (and regionally) on every calendar day from 1990 to present using an
     O(N log N) event sweep-line algorithm (+1 on discovery date, -1 on day
     after fire-out date, cumulative sum over sorted daily index).
   - Bucket daily concurrency into 5 load tiers (`Low <50`, `Moderate 50-150`,
     `High 150-300`, `Severe 300-500`, `Extreme 500+`).

5. Control 4 — Physical Fire Weather Severity Index (`WEATHER_SEVERITY_SCORE`):
   - Combine standardized scores of Max VPD, Max Wind Speed, 30-Day Antecedent
     Rainfall Deficit, and Elevation into a 0–100 composite physical difficulty
     index, split into 3 tiers (`Mild`, `Moderate`, `Extreme Fire Weather`).

2.3 PHASE 2: COUNTERFACTUAL EFFICIENCY MODELING (`src/counterfactual_model.py`)
Instead of relying only on raw ratios (`Days / Acre`), Codex must implement a
Counterfactual Baseline Framework:

1. Define Baseline Era (1990–2005) and Modern Era (2015–present).
2. Train two Gradient Boosted Regressors on the 1990–2005 Baseline Era to
   predict `SUPP_DAYS`:
   - `Model_Naive`: Uses only `log(TOTALACRES)`, Latitude, Longitude, and Day of Year.
   - `Model_Full_Control`: Uses `log(TOTALACRES)`, Coordinates, Day of Year,
     Ignition Cause, WUI Distance, `CONCURRENT_FIRES`, VPD, Wind Speed, 30-Day
     Drought Deficit, and Elevation.
3. Apply both baseline models to all fires from 1990 to present and calculate:
   - `EFFICIENCY_DEFICIT_DAYS = Actual SUPP_DAYS - Expected SUPP_DAYS (1990s Model)`
4. Attribution Decomposition:
   - Compare the deficit from `Model_Naive` vs. `Model_Full_Control` to quantify
     exactly what percentage of modern suppression slowdown is explained by:
     (a) Hotter/drier fire weather (VPD/wind/drought),
     (b) Higher simultaneous fire concurrency (resource bottlenecks),
     (c) Wilderness "let-burn" policy shifts, versus
     (d) Unexplained operational slowdown.
5. Train a parallel Modern Era Regressor (2015–present) and serialize all models
   so users can run live counterfactual comparisons in the dashboard.
